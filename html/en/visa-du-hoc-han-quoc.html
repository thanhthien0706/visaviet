Korea student visa (D2, D4) 2026: Differences & requirements for each type

Last updated: 12/05/2026
By: Thành Phạm

Korea student visas are currently divided into two main types: the D2 visa for students on degree programmes at universities, and the D4 visa for international students learning Korean or in vocational training. Each has a different purpose and duration, so you need to choose the one that fits your study path.

Common types of Korea student visa

International students currently have two main student visas to choose from:

  • D2 visa: For students on degree programmes at Korean universities, including bachelor's, master's and doctoral programmes. It is a long-term visa that allows a stay of several years, with the chance to switch to other visa types after graduation to work or settle.
  • D4 visa: For international students at language schools or vocational training institutions. It is usually the first choice for those who do not yet have a Korean language certificate and want to learn the language before entering a degree programme.

Each type has a different purpose and duration, so you need to understand them in order to choose the one that fits your study path.

Detailed comparison of the D2 and D4 visas

Criterion Visa D4 Visa D2
Who it is for Students on Korean language courses at language institutes affiliated with universities and colleges. Students with an official admission letter to a major (college, bachelor's, master's, doctorate).
Purpose Learn Korean to meet the language entry requirement for a degree programme. Study a major and do academic research.
Duration Usually 6 months to 1 year, extended in Korea according to the length of the course. Usually 2 to 4 years depending on the programme, extended every year in Korea.
Language requirement TOPIK not required (but it is an advantage). At least TOPIK 3 for bachelor's and TOPIK 4 for master's programmes (or an equivalent English certificate).
Part-time work Allowed after 6 months in Korea, up to 20 hours per week. Allowed as soon as you have the alien registration card, up to 20-25 hours per week (depending on the programme).

Basically, most Vietnamese international students first apply for a D4 visa to learn the language, then, once they reach the required TOPIK level, switch to a D2 visa in Korea without returning to Vietnam.

Requirements for a Korea student visa

To apply for a Korea student visa, you need to meet these basic requirements:

Criterion Requirements
Education
  • Graduated from high school no more than 3 years ago.
  • Graduated from vocational school, college or university no more than 05 years ago.
  • A 3-year high school grade point average (GPA) of preferably 6.5 or higher.
Health
  • Healthy enough to study and live in Korea.
  • Free of dangerous infectious diseases, especially pulmonary tuberculosis
    (mandatory; a health certificate from a designated hospital is required).
Personal background
  • A Vietnamese citizen with no relatives (in the household register) staying illegally in Korea.
  • No criminal record or prior offences.
  • Not banned from leaving Vietnam or from entering Korea.
Finances
  • The family can afford tuition and living costs for the whole period of study.
  • This is one of the most important factors and must be clearly proven in the application.

Korea student visa documents 2025

A Korea student visa application usually includes:

  • Visa application form (standard form). See detailed instructions here
  • A passport valid for more than 6 months.
  • A 3.5 x 4.5cm photo. See detailed instructions here
  • Personal documents: the student's and parents' citizen ID cards, birth certificate, household register.
  • Graduation diploma and school report/transcript (originals).
  • Proof of finances (savings book, income documents, land use right certificate if any).
  • Admission notice or invitation letter from the Korean school.
  • Health certificate as required.

Depending on the visa type (D2 or D4), Korean language certificates or other required documents may be added.

Korea student visa procedure from A to Z

The Korea student visa process can be divided into these main stages:

  1. Research and choose a school: Learn about universities, colleges, Korean language courses and majors that suit you. Consider factors such as entry requirements, tuition, location and the school's reputation.
  2. Prepare your school application: After choosing a school, prepare a complete application to send to it to request an admission letter.
  3. Get an admission letter (or visa code): The school will review your application. If you qualify, it will send an admission letter or obtain a visa code for you. Visa codes are usually for top-ranked schools and make applying for the visa in Vietnam easier.
  4. Prepare your visa application: Based on the admission letter/visa code, prepare the application to submit to the Korean Embassy or Consulate in Vietnam.
  5. Submit and interview (if required): Submit your application at the Korea Visa Application Center (KVAC) and attend an interview (depending on the case).
  6. Receive your visa result: After the review period, you will receive your visa result.

Tips on proving finances for a student visa

Here are some tips on proving your finances for study in Korea, so that the consulate can see you are able to pay for your studies:

  • Open a savings account early enough: Prepare a savings book of at least US$10,000 – 20,000 (depending on the school), opened at least 3 months before the submission date.
  • A transparent source of funds: Avoid depositing a large sum within a short time just before applying, as it may raise suspicion.
  • Have a financial sponsor: If you use your parents' account, you need documents proving the relationship and their income (labour contract, salary statements, business licence…).
  • Choose a school on the priority list: For top 1% schools or direct visas, the financial proof requirements may be lighter, improving the approval rate.
  • Prepare supporting documents: Such as certificates of ownership of assets or property, or other income documents, to make your application more convincing.

Latest Korea student visa costs 2026

The total cost of a student visa includes several different fees. Below are indicative amounts for 2026 [1] :

  • Visa fee (paid to the Consulate): About US$80. This fee may change over time; you pay it in VND at the set exchange rate.
  • KVAC service fee: About VND 390,000 per application. This fee is mandatory when you submit through the center [2] .
  • Translation and notarisation: About VND 1,000,000 – 2,000,000, depending on the number of documents.
  • Health examination: About VND 800,000 – 1,000,000, depending on the hospital.

Note: These costs do not include postage for sending documents (if any) or other extra expenses.

Korea student visa processing time

A Korea student visa application usually takes about 15 – 30 working days to process (excluding Saturdays, Sundays and public holidays).

Common Korea student visa interview questions

If you are called for an interview, don't worry too much. It is your chance to present yourself and your genuine purpose of study. The questions usually fall into 4 main groups:

Questions about you and your family:

  • Please introduce yourself.
  • How many people are in your family? What do your parents do?
  • Why do you want to study in Korea rather than another country?

Questions about your studies:

  • When did you graduate? What were your grades?
  • Why did you choose school A and major B?
  • What do you know about your school and major?
  • What is your study plan in Korea? (Your answer should match the study plan you wrote).

Questions about finances:

  • Who is paying for your study abroad?
  • What is your parents' monthly income?
  • Do you know that tuition and living costs in Korea are expensive?

Questions about future plans:

  • What will you do after you finish your studies?
  • Do you intend to stay and work in Korea? (The safest answer is that you will return to Vietnam to build your career).
  • Do you have relatives in Korea?

Answer briefly, honestly and confidently, consistent with the information in your application. A polite attitude, smart clothing and the ability to communicate in Korean (even just basic greetings) will be a big plus.

Common reasons student visa applications are refused

Quite a few Korea student visa applications are refused even though they were fairly well prepared. The reasons are usually the following:

  • Dishonest application: Forged documents and false information are the most serious reasons for refusal and can get you banned from reapplying for a long time.
  • Unclear purpose of study: A sketchy study plan, weak grades or hesitant interview answers that fail to show a real wish to study.
  • Weak finances: Savings that are not strong enough, or no transparent proof of the sponsor's income.
  • Past violations or an unclear record: A previous visa refusal, or signs of false information or forged documents.
  • Immigration history: If you have relatives staying illegally in Korea, or a “black mark” on your own immigration record, refusal is likely.

To reduce the risk, prepare an honest application, prove your finances clearly and have a concrete study plan.

Frequently asked questions

What is the main difference between the D2 and D4 visas?

The main difference between the D2 and D4 visas is who they are for and the purpose of study. The D4 visa is for students learning Korean at universities or language institutes, while the D2 visa is for formal degree studies (college, bachelor's, master's…).

How much money do I need to show to study in Korea?

To study in Korea, the required proof of funds is usually US$9,000 – 10,000 in a savings book, deposited at a bank at least 3 months in advance, to show that you can pay tuition and living costs in the initial period.

Is the Korea student visa interview difficult?

If you have a clear purpose of study, an honest application and well-prepared answers, the interview will not be too difficult. What matters most is confidence and sincerity.

If my application is refused, can I reapply?

You are allowed to reapply. However, you usually have to wait 3 – 6 months from the date of the refusal. During this time, find out why you were refused so you can fix the problem and improve your application.

Are international students allowed to work part-time in Korea?

Yes, but you need to register with your school and the immigration office. Language students (D4) may work part-time after 6 months in Korea. Degree students (D2) may work part-time as soon as they have the alien registration card. The maximum is 20-25 hours per week, with no limit during vacations.

Do I need to buy a flight ticket before the visa is issued?

It is not required. It is best to buy your ticket after the visa is issued to avoid risk.
